# Thief: next design direction

Status: **unreleased design candidate**. This is a handoff for the next Thief implementation pass, not a description of the published Thief 1.0.0 package or an approved balance specification. The [original design](DESIGN.md), [combat rules](COMBAT.md), [equipment ledger](EQUIPMENT.md), and [artifact rules](ARTIFACTS.md) preserve the published bow-era baseline. The advanced development candidate replaces that secondary bow path with compact Talent flintlocks while retaining the internal `thief_bow_*` item IDs for save identity. Its art and package have not passed release acceptance. Integrate and validate that candidate before changing published documentation or catalog data.

## Player promise and choice

Keep the Thief a quick, fragile opportunist. A teammate's direct hit, a foe that has not yet acted, or a deliberately Prepared attack creates a chance for one precise strike. Paired daggers remain the reliable Speed path with Twin Feint. The candidate flintlock path offers a different four-check Talent investment, ammunition and reload decisions, and one native damage result per shot. Slip Away still costs a full turn. The new endgame choice should be **which risk the Thief accepts to improve preparation, opening burst, or field survival**, not a general damage upgrade on top of all three.

The three existing final-family looks and names are retained:

| Family | Existing visual and gear identity | Proposed armor-role direction | Inseparable cost to tune |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Locksmith | Teal/gold keys, reinforced coat, preparation weapons, tool and Focus charm | Make a Prepared strike more resource-efficient or easier to carry to the next turn. Preserve the Skeleton Key artifact's distinct repeatable refund. | Lower direct damage or opening burst while this role is active. A free Focus engine is out of scope. |
| Nightblade | Dark wine cloth, hooked paired blades, high Speed and raw weapon damage | Reward choosing an uninjured or not-yet-acted target for a decisive opening. | Weaker magical defense and less preparation/penetration reliability. Candle's End's once-per-combat 75% opening must replace, never stack with, a role bonus. |
| Wayfarer | Moss and wood field gear, compass motifs, Farstep flintlock | Improve repeatable positioning and survival after an eligible direct attack; retain a useful baseline with paired daggers. A matching pistol may add a further completion payoff. | Less peak Sneak Attack damage or Focus efficiency. The Unlost Road's Evasion reward must use the stronger value, never add both. |

These role effects, exact percentages, resource limits, and even whether a specific effect belongs to armor or a weapon are **unapproved Thief proposals**. A first tuning envelope is at most one extra Focus actually spent and returned per combat for Locksmith, about 5-10 percentage points of weapon damage shifted into Nightblade's qualified opening, and roughly 4 Evasion until the next turn for Wayfarer. These are comparison targets, not content values. Measure them against neutral Thief, strongest mixed gear, Hunter's bow/initiative role, and Treasure Hunter's firearm/utility role before adoption. Do not copy Paladin's Guard, healing, ward, or Smite identity.

## Matching visible armor

Proposed evaluator: count only the equipped **body, head, and feet** pieces in each of the Locksmith, Nightblade, and Wayfarer families. Two matching visible armor pieces grant that family's small role preview; all three grant its core benefit and its full drawback. A third piece from another family gives its intrinsic item stats but never completes the three-piece core. At most one family profile is active. The matching charm, daggers, pistol, and artifact are neither required to unlock the three-armor core nor allowed to complete a missing armor slot. A matching weapon can provide a separate completion benefit after the three-armor core, if the benefit survives balance review. This preserves visible character identity and lets the player change weapons without losing the chosen armor role.

Keep role benefits and costs in one profile transaction. Do not combine the strongest benefit of each worn piece, preserve a stale profile after a swap, or let a trinket bypass an armor requirement. Intrinsic item stats remain available to any wearer. Thief-only profile effects require the Thief class; other classes still receive ordinary item stats and attacks. The evaluator must handle 0/1/2/3 pieces, mixed armor, two-hand weapon swaps, artifacts, unequipped slots, non-Thief wearers, save/reload, and combat avatar rebuilding. The 2-piece threshold, 3-piece core, and example effects above are proposed mechanics, not implemented current behavior. The historical statement in [DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md) that mixing is encouraged and there are no set bonuses remains true of the published baseline; this proposed role system would supersede it only after implementation and validation.

## Class and weapon action ownership

Retain Opportunist, Sneak Attack, Twin Feint, Prepared, and Slip Away as Thief-class capabilities with their existing timing and counterplay. Sneak Attack and Twin Feint should use an explicit stable-ID list of eligible paired weapons; any pistol eligibility must also use exact authored IDs after its combat semantics are proven. Do not infer eligibility from a broad weapon type, name, model, or visual. A stale combat button or committed action must recheck current class and equipment. Slip Away is intentionally equipment-independent. Native `dualKnife` and `dualDagger` eligibility and their ownership gates remain separate exact cases.

Ordinary weapon attacks stay on the weapons and remain usable by other classes. Their class-specific Prepared or Sneak Attack payoff does not transfer with the weapon. In the advanced candidate, each pistol occupies both equipment hands, tests Talent with four checks, consumes native ammunition on Fire and the special shot, and lets only a Thief with an eligible pistol restore one round at the start of their combat turn. Other classes use the native firearm reload cycle. Those are development-candidate declarations, not shipped or fully live-verified behavior. Do not turn every Feint, Bait Shot, Pierce, or Deadeye button into a class action merely because its class payoff is restricted. Preserve native white outlined combat icons and check enabled, disabled, and selected states; avoid colored generated action art.

Give the class, owned set section, and item card short structured text: current armor count, active benefit **and cost**, next threshold, matching-weapon completion if any, and the exact eligibility of class effects. The item card must not imply a pistol is a bow or that a non-Thief receives Instant Reload. Keep action descriptions distinct from base weapon damage and native ammo/reload requirements. Validate normal and Shift/extra-detail faces, backpack and equipped cards, font size, footer prompts, and restoration when the card closes or the loadout changes.

## Shared acquisition direction

Use the same transferable **Guild Token** currency and native Back Alley vendor service planned for Paladin, not a Thief-only token or a bespoke merchant UI. In that candidate, admitted native enemy rewards at displayed combat level 8 or higher have a provisional 10% ordinary token chance; reviewed named-boss groups use 50% instead, and the sixth consecutive eligible opportunity without a token guarantees one. Those rates are shared pacing candidates, not measured Thief supply. Reward ordering, boss grouping, inventory ownership, save/resume, and co-op authority must be deterministic. A class is not required in the party for token drops. The current shared candidate disables online purchases; retain that guard until authority, synchronized payment and delivery, reconnect, and duplicate-request behavior have explicit support and tests.

The proposed Thief catalog contains the eighteen ordinary final-family items: three families times body, head, feet, paired daggers, flintlock, and charm. Each chosen piece costs one token in an eligible town's Back Alley. Use the native vendor stock, item card, affordability display, buyer identity, confirmation, and backpack delivery. The no-refund confirmation is an irreversible token-to-item purchase; native resale is a separate action and never a token refund. The three visible armor pieces cost three tokens to complete a core; a matching weapon is an optional fourth purchase. Artifacts remain outside the exchange and retain their separate acquisition policy. If this catalog is adopted, remove those eighteen items' ordinary final-band random-drop and merchant declarations only after the shared exchange route is working, so no item becomes unobtainable during integration.

The published Thief equipment already has a **Guild Token** charm (`thief_charm_guild`). That is a wearable item, not the shared currency. Preserve its stable item ID for saves. Before release, choose a distinct display name for the charm and update its card, art captions, and migration text; do not rename the shared currency or silently convert owned charms into spendable tokens.

## Art and evidence standard

Preserve strong original geometry. Fix coats at their actual skinning, topology, normals, UV and material source; do not hide holes, shell breakthrough, bulky hood proportions or poor grips by repainting studio art. Inspect cloth, leather, metal and ornament under native lighting, with equipped and item-display materials checked separately. Follow the active model-budget policy and measure the complete equipped loadout. Minor native-like contact is acceptable; distorting the silhouette to eliminate every contact is not. Keep paired grips and pistol muzzle orientation grounded in native animation conventions.

Use Astra High for every image-generation and modeling task. Generate accepted-source portraits only after native model review. Each tier and artifact needs a concise studio-versus-equipped comparison tied to exact source, texture, runtime and content identities. Label gameplay screenshots, native item-camera renders, diagnostics and studio art distinctly. Reuse prior evidence only where the exact assets and relevant behavior remain unchanged. Complete front/side/back standing and relevant movement/combat coverage for each supported wearer; resolve observed defects before expanding repetitive captures. Maintain evidence-backed pipeline recommendations, including promotional consistency, without changing shared skills in this pass.

## Implementation order and acceptance

1. Reconcile the advanced candidate with the released branch: preserve stable 1.0.0 item IDs, inspect native pistol ammunition/reload/animation behavior, and update the package's real framework/helper minimum only when that support is integrated. The Paladin exchange and equipment APIs are still uncommitted in another development line; integrate reviewed shared primitives selectively through `Content.*` rather than copying a whole worktree or placing engine patches in Thief content. Paladin's `SetGuardianProfile` and `AddGuardianEquipmentSet` contracts are Guardian-specific and assume hammer/shield slots; they cannot be reused unchanged for Thief's paired-dagger/pistol roles. Reuse generic visible-armor counting only where valid, then add a small Thief profile and armament schema without granting Guardian capability or inventing shield slots.
2. Audit every cloned weapon proficiency, icon, break fragment, modifier and firearm field. Verify no inherited area attack, free reload, hidden armor bypass, extra hit, or class skill leak. Define the exact Thief armor evaluator, profile reset, matching-armament rule, and class/weapon ID registry with deterministic identities and authoritative commit checks. Keep specific role coefficients explicitly provisional and report the tradeoffs before treating them as accepted balance.
3. Integrate the shared token reward and Back Alley service. Test displayed-level 7/8 boundaries, ordinary and named-boss chances, the sixth-opportunity guarantee, multipart bosses, stable multiplayer ordering, individual inventory ownership, buying with 0/1 tokens, chosen-row delivery, cancel/confirm, no refund, normal Market restoration, and save/reload. Offline registration cannot establish these native flows.
4. Test neutral, each 2-piece preview, each 3-piece core, matching weapon completion, mixed high-stat loadouts, artifact substitution, non-Thief wearers, and mid-combat swaps. Record actual attack damage, Focus spent/refunded, ammo and reload turns, Evasion and damage prevented, item action availability, combat turn count, and losses against comparable native classes. Reject a role that wins both sustained output and survival without paying its stated cost.
5. Reconcile models and media only after mechanics settle: original and editable sources, exact equipped/display renderer paths, both blades, pistol grip/muzzle/projectile timing, cloth shell and material behavior, both baseline wearers, female and male gait, attack and damage animations, alternate appearances, and inventory/vendor presentation. Studio images and standing poses do not establish combat fit. Preserve rejected coat experiments as evidence, and adopt a corrected coat only after native motion and budget checks.

This document changes design direction only. No candidate bonus, pistol compatibility, token delivery, native UI, co-op, or full art acceptance is claimed by this text. Website impact: the published Thief pages, generated `website/src/data/thief.json`, item cards and media, guide, catalog/download metadata, framework requirement, and release notes stay tied to the released bow package. Update them from the approved immutable package at release, using the website generators and visual/accessibility checks; do not silently present this unreleased pistol and set design as shipped.
